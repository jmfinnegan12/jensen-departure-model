Will Jensen Huang leave as Nvidia CEO?

Question Model

GitHub · suggest a change · how to contribute

Resolved Nov 12, 2026. Nvidia announced that Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately.29 The page below is the forecast as it stood on Nov 11.

Base case

Before this month, the chance of Huang leaving in a given year was low single digits. He co-founded Nvidia, has run it for more than three decades, and said in January he will stay "for as long as I deserve it".5 The model adjusts from there.

The affair, and the allegations

On Nov 8, 2026 Huang told Nvidia staff he had been unfaithful to his wife.2 The woman he had the affair with is reported to be an executive of Megaspeed, an Nvidia customer, whom U.S. intelligence officials reportedly consider an asset of China's Ministry of State Security.3 Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation.1

The affair needs to be separated from the allegations that she is a Chinese spy and an executive of a customer. The affair, admitted at once, is unlikely to cost him the job on its own. The allegations could.

On Nov 11 The New York Times reported a second relationship, with Kimberly Powell, an Nvidia vice president who reports directly to him; Nvidia declined to comment.25 The same day The Wall Street Journal reported that the board is preparing to oust him.26

The critical question

If the dealings were above board, he has a very high probability of staying. If there was a conflict of interest, a preference given, or information passed, keeping his job would be a long shot. So the critical question is the chance that something improper happened and comes to light. If nothing is found, nothing comes to light. Since Nov 11 the answer is close to yes: a second relationship, with a direct report, has been reported, and the board is reported to be preparing to oust him.

The forecast

Exits of this kind come fast or not at all. Since Nov 11, most of the risk falls in the coming days and weeks. If he is still CEO after that, the chance falls back toward the base case.

Why these numbers
Chance that something improper happened and comes to light
My gut feeling is that an affair between a chief executive and an executive of one of his company's customers more likely than not leads to something improper coming to light. It has to be found and made public, not only to have happened, which is why this is not higher. On Nov 11 I lowered it from 60% to 57%: a lot of voices support Jensen and him staying, among them the President, Elon Musk, prominent investors, customers and most of his employees. I read these as tailwinds for Jensen and headwinds for an exit. Later that day I raised it to 98%. The second relationship, with a subordinate, is something else coming out, more serious than an affair with a customer in a vacuum, and it now represents a pattern of behavior. The New York Times and The Wall Street Journal pride themselves on verified, if sometimes unnamed, sources; they are not in the business of unverified gossip.
Chance he leaves, if something improper comes to light
Keeping his job after that would be a long shot. Given the stakes of the AI race with China it should be higher than the base rate for chief executives with findings against them, but 95% felt too high. On Nov 11 I raised it to 97%, with 95% to 99% as the range, given The Wall Street Journal's report that the board is preparing to oust him. Of 11 chief executives found to have had a relationship with an employee, 10 left, among them the heads of Intel, McDonald's and BP;27 that base rate is low for this case, a repeat on top of a national security spy allegation. His status as founder and the lack of a succession plan change things, and keep this short of certain.
Chance he leaves over the scandal, if nothing more comes to light
If the dealings were above board, he has a very high probability of staying. I put this below the base rate, given his commitment, founder status, long tenure and relative youth.
Median days until he has left, if he leaves over the scandal
Since Nov 11: a week, counted from Nov 11, with 21 days as the slow case and a day as the fast case. This is moving faster than the 10 days I first gave that day. The pattern of multiple affairs, each with a problematic person, and the news that the board is moving to oust him point to an even faster base rate. In past cases where a chief executive's relationship leaked before the board acted, the median from the first report to the exit was about 11 days, and in nearly every case he left the day the exit was announced.27 The time to a decision and the time from a decision to his actual departure are now one input.
Before Nov 11 this was the median days until the decisive finding or report: 90 days. The starting point was 85, the median in my count of 11 past board investigations of a sitting chief executive; the list is not yet published. On Nov 10 the board hired a former acting U.S. attorney, Joon H. Kim, to run its investigation.8 His two earlier public investigations, of New York's governor and of the FDIC, each took about five months.9,10 The board's investigation and the FBI's should be expected to take months. The path to an early exit runs mainly through the press: more information leaked, discovered and published. The market's first date, Jan 1, is a little more than a month and a half away, well under the median length of an investigation. The fast case is 55 days; the slow case, 120, is a placeholder.
Days from the decisive finding or report until he has actually left
Merged into the timing input above on Nov 11. Before that: 14 days, with 7 as the fast case and 25 as the slow case.
Chance per year he leaves for a reason unrelated to the scandal
Low single digits. He is a co-founder with more than three decades in the job who says he will stay "for as long as I deserve it".5 This covers mortality and the tail risk of extremely poor performance.
Assumptions
  • An exit caused by the scandal follows the same clock whether or not something improper comes to light.
  • Half of the exits over the scandal come by the median, three quarters by twice the median, and so on.
  • Unrelated exits happen at a constant yearly rate, independent of the scandal.
Weak points
  • Two reports, anonymous sources. The second relationship and the board's plan to oust him rest on unnamed sources in The New York Times and The Wall Street Journal; no other outlet has confirmed either independently, and Nvidia has not commented.25,26
  • One clock. A single median spreads the chance of an exit evenly over time. A board meeting in the coming days would concentrate it.
  • There is almost no precedent. No founder-CEO case with a relationship with a direct report was found, and a chief executive tied to an alleged agent of an adversary state has few earlier cases to compare with.
  • The President's backing. He has said Huang should stay.3 If the tide turns against Huang, I expect him to go with the tide.
  • Only an actual departure counts. An announced future exit does not, and the contract terms do not address a leave of absence or an interim chief executive.4 A leave would leave the near dates in doubt.
What would change the forecast
  • A board decision: removal, resignation, a leave of absence or an interim chief executive.
  • Nvidia, Huang or Kimberly Powell confirming or denying the second relationship.
  • Nvidia starting to put a succession plan in place.
  • Huang's answer to the House chairs' request for a closed briefing on Nov 18, or a subpoena.6,14
  • Public hearings, a subpoena, a review of Nvidia's export licences, or charges in the Megaspeed case.
  • New reporting on what he knew, or on what passed.

  • Did the board know of it before Nov 8?
  • Did Megaspeed receive anything other customers did not?
  • What is the FBI examining?
  • Did the board know of the relationship with Kimberly Powell, and was the bystander who filmed them offered a non-disclosure agreement, as reported?25

  • Would a leave of absence count as leaving?
